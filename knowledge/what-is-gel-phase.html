Knowledge / Soapmaking

What is gel phase in soapmaking?

Short answer

It's a self-heating stage where fresh soap reaches 54–82 °C and turns semi-translucent, which speeds saponification.

Explanation

The reaction is exothermic, so fresh soap heats itself. In gel phase it reaches 54–82 °C (130–180 °F), becomes semi-transparent and jelly-like, and saponification reaches about 80–90% within hours.

Full gel gives brighter colors (micas, clays, spirulina, charcoal), a glossy, slightly translucent finish and a harder, longer-lasting bar, with a standard 4–6 week cure. No-gel soap needs about 6–8 weeks. To check afterwards, look at the soap 12–24 hours after molding: gelled soap is firm, warm and slightly translucent, ungelled soap softer and cooler.

What to do

  • To encourage gel: use more saturated fats (coconut), soap warmer (40–50 °C) to medium-heavy trace, and insulate with towels or a warm, switched-off oven.
  • To prevent gel: soap at room temperature to thin trace, then put the mold straight into the freezer, or use small individual molds.
  • Skip gel for milk soaps and layered designs.

Watch out for

  • Sugars, honey, milk and some spices raise the heat. Watch closely when oven-gelling to avoid cracking or 'volcanoing'.

Source: The Art of Personal Soapmaking — The Gel Phase in Cold & Hot Process Soapmaking

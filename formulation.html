The emulsion timeline

From the Barrier Repair Cream method. Each temperature exists to protect something.

  1. 70–72 °CHeat oil and water phases separately; hold the water phase about 5 minutes
  2. 70–72 °CPour oil into water in a thin stream; mix on high 3–4 minutes
  3. 38–40 °CAdd actives one at a time while cooling
  4. 35 °CAdd the preservative; mix 1–2 minutes; check the pH
  5. 25–28 °CStop stirring, pack, and rest 24 hours

The sterilization chain

  1. Clean in hot soapy water.
  2. Rinse with distilled or very hot water.
  3. Sterilize or disinfect: pressure cooker (15 min at 15 PSI), dry-heat oven (160 °C, 2+ hours), or 70% isopropyl alcohol and air-dry. Never towel-dry.
  4. Sanitize containers with alcohol just before filling.

How should I approach formulation as a beginner?

Learn the protocol, then learn what each ingredient does and why it goes in at a particular phase and temperature, before you start changing recipes.

Why is pH important in skincare?

Healthy skin's acid mantle sits at about pH 4.5–5.5. Products in that range protect the barrier, and pH also decides whether some actives and preservatives work.

The Art of Natural Skincare

Formulation

How do I adjust the pH of a formula?

Drop by drop, with a 10% acid solution to lower it or a 10% sodium hydroxide solution to raise it, checking after each addition.

Formulation

What is an emulsion, and how do I make a cream?

A cream is an oil-in-water emulsion: oil droplets held evenly through a water phase by an emulsifier. Both phases are heated to the same temperature and combined under high shear.

The Art of Natural Skincare

Formulation

What are the phases in a formula?

Groups of ingredients handled together at the same stage: water phase, oil phase, actives, preservative and pH adjustment.

Formulation

What's the difference between a serum and a cream?

The books don't define the terms side by side, but their formulas show the difference: the serum is a water-based gel, while the creams are emulsions of oil and water.

Formulation

What are active ingredients, and when do I add them?

Actives are the ingredients chosen for a specific effect, such as panthenol, peptides or niacinamide. Most are heat-sensitive, so they go into the cooling product at 38–40 °C.

The Art of Natural Skincare

Formulation

Do I need a preservative?

Any product containing water does. Anhydrous products (oils, butters, scrubs, bath salts) don't, as long as water never gets in.

The Art of Natural Skincare

Formulation

How do I make a gel without lumps?

Pre-disperse natural gums in glycerin before adding them to water, and sprinkle polymer powders slowly while stirring.

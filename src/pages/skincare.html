The four foundations

  • Hygiene: sterilized tools, a disinfected workspace, gloves.
  • Preservation: anything with water needs a broad-spectrum preservative.
  • pH: match skin's acid mantle, about 4.5–5.5.
  • Patch test: inner arm, 24–48 hours, every new product.

How a formula is built

Every formula in the book groups ingredients into phases, each added at its own moment:

  • A Water or hydrosol phase
  • B Oil phase with the emulsifier
  • C Heat-sensitive actives, added at 38–40 °C
  • D Preservation, at about 35 °C
  • E pH adjustment, drop by drop

Three kinds of product

Emulsions (the creams) bring oil and water together with an emulsifier, both phases heated to 70–72 °C and combined under high shear. Gels and cleansers are water-based, built at room temperature with gums, polymers or mild surfactants. Anhydrous products (facial oils, butters, scrubs, bath salts) contain no water, so they need no preservative, only antioxidants and completely dry tools.

A sample from the book: Calming Chamomile Foam Cleanser

The book's first formula: a pH-balanced foaming cleanser for sensitive skin, mixed without heat or emulsifying. Yield about 186 g; target pH 4.5–5.5; shelf life 9 months.

  1. Dissolve Phase A, warming gently to 30–35 °C if needed.
  2. Combine the Phase B and C surfactants, extracts, silk, betaine and caprylyl glycol.
  3. Pour into Phase A slowly while stirring, to avoid foam.
  4. Premix polysorbate 80 with the lavender oil until clear, then add.
  5. Add the preservative and adjust the pH drop by drop.
  6. Bottle in a foaming pump and rest 24 hours.
Before you use it

Work to the hygiene protocol, and patch test on your inner arm for 24–48 hours before first use.
